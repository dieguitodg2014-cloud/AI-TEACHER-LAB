# RUNTIME TEST 06 - SAME TOPIC, DIFFERENT LEVEL

## Purpose

Validate that AI TEACHER LAB changes the pedagogical demand of the same topic across A0, A1, A2, B1, and B2 rather than merely changing vocabulary or sentence length.

## Test Topic

**Topic:** Personal experiences and travel

**Communicative domain:** talking about experiences, asking about experiences, describing what happened, and discussing what was learned.

The topic remains constant. The pedagogical demand changes.

## A0

**Objective:** Students identify and produce very short supported statements about familiar personal experiences.

**Language demand:** Highly controlled chunks such as "I went to...", "I visited...", "I liked...", supported by pictures, models, and a visible word bank.

**Cognitive demand:** Recognition and reproduction with simple personal choice.

**Interaction:** Teacher modeling followed by tightly scaffolded pair exchange.

**Scaffolding:** Very high.

**Expected output:** Words, chunks, and short sentences.

**Communicative task:** Students choose from familiar picture prompts and tell a partner one thing they did or liked. Partner asks one previously taught question.

**Evidence:** Student communicates the intended basic meaning intelligibly using the taught support.

**QC observation:** The task cannot depend on English that has not been explicitly taught.

## A1

**Objective:** Students exchange simple information about past experiences in predictable situations.

**Language demand:** Simple past questions and answers, basic time expressions, familiar travel/activity vocabulary.

**Cognitive demand:** Selection and basic application.

**Interaction:** Guided pair interview with a limited question set and optional support box.

**Scaffolding:** High but reduced compared with A0.

**Expected output:** Short sentences and short exchanges.

**Communicative task:** Students interview a partner about three experiences and record key information.

**Evidence:** Student can ask and answer basic questions and communicate understandable information about familiar past experiences.

## A2

**Objective:** Students describe personal experiences using simple connected language and add relevant details.

**Language demand:** Past forms plus connectors such as because, but, so, and when; selected experience-related language.

**Cognitive demand:** Combination and organization of information.

**Interaction:** Information-gap pair task followed by a short exchange about similarities and differences.

**Scaffolding:** Moderate.

**Expected output:** A short connected account rather than isolated answers.

**Communicative task:** Students compare two experiences and explain which one they preferred and why.

**Evidence:** Student completes the task with connected language, understandable organization, and functional grammatical control.

## B1

**Objective:** Students narrate and compare experiences, explain reasons and consequences, and maintain an interaction with follow-up questions.

**Language demand:** Connected discourse with purposeful use of past forms, Present Perfect where relevant, sequencing language, reasons, consequences, and descriptive detail.

**Cognitive demand:** Analysis and application.

**Interaction:** Semi-open pair or small-group discussion with no complete script. Learners must ask follow-up questions and respond to unexpected but manageable comments.

**Scaffolding:** Selective. Prompt cards provide themes, not complete sentences.

**Expected output:** Sustained connected discourse with several linked ideas.

**Communicative task:** Students discuss a memorable experience, explain what made it significant, compare it with another experience, and respond to classmates' follow-up questions. The group then chooses the most interesting experience and justifies the decision.

**Evidence:** Task achievement, coherence, fluency, range/control, and ability to maintain interaction.

**QC observation:** This is not an A2 task made longer. The learner must narrate, explain, compare, justify, listen actively, and manage follow-up interaction.

## B2

**Objective:** Students discuss experiences critically, evaluate their impact, justify interpretations, and adapt language to the interaction and register.

**Language demand:** Flexible use of complex and varied structures, precise vocabulary and collocation, cohesive discourse, and appropriate formal/informal choices.

**Cognitive demand:** Analysis, evaluation, and synthesis.

**Interaction:** Extended discussion or debate with spontaneous responses and competing viewpoints.

**Scaffolding:** Minimal and strategic.

**Expected output:** Extended, nuanced discourse with qualification, justification, comparison, and synthesis.

**Communicative task:** Students discuss whether travel and unfamiliar experiences genuinely change people. They use personal or observed experiences as evidence, challenge or qualify classmates' ideas, and collaboratively formulate a nuanced conclusion.

**Evidence:** Effectiveness and precision, coherence/cohesion, fluency and interaction management, range/control, register, and pragmatic appropriacy.

**QC observation:** B2 demand is not created by simply adding difficult vocabulary. The task requires evaluation, argumentation, synthesis, spontaneous interaction, precision, and nuance.

## Cross-Level Validation Matrix

| Dimension | A0 | A1 | A2 | B1 | B2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Objective | Basic supported meaning | Basic exchange | Connected description | Narration, comparison, explanation | Critical discussion and evaluation |
| Cognitive demand | Recognition/reproduction | Selection/application | Combination | Analysis/application | Analysis/evaluation/synthesis |
| Scaffolding | Very high | High | Moderate | Selective | Minimal/strategic |
| Interaction | Controlled | Guided | Semi-guided | Increasingly free | Mostly spontaneous |
| Output | Chunks/simple sentences | Short exchanges | Connected short discourse | Sustained discourse | Extended nuanced discourse |
| Autonomy | Very low | Low | Moderate | High | High |
| Assessment | Intelligibility/task success | Functional communication | Connected task completion | Coherence/fluency/interaction | Precision/cohesion/register/pragmatics |

## Result

**STATUS: PASS**

The five versions preserve the broad topic while changing the objective, cognitive demand, interaction, scaffolding, autonomy, output, language expectations, and assessment criteria.

The test therefore supports the Level Control Engine rule that adaptation must not be limited to vocabulary difficulty.
