# RUNTIME TEST 04 - MODO ESL B1

## Test Input

`Modo ESL: Present Perfect B1`

## Test Purpose

Validate that the integrated system converts the Modo ESL activation plus B1 Level Control requirements into a genuinely B1 lesson, rather than an A2 lesson with harder vocabulary or longer sentences.

## System Parameters Derived Before Design

- Target level: B1
- Learner profile: adult/intermediate learners able to communicate independently in familiar and moderately unfamiliar situations
- Duration: 90 minutes
- Communicative objective: learners can talk about life experiences and recent experiences, ask relevant follow-up questions, compare experiences, and explain or justify responses.
- Language objective: use Present Perfect for life experience and relevant recent experience, with contrast to Past Simple when a finished past time is specified.
- Cognitive demand: selection of relevant experiences, comparison, explanation, follow-up questioning, and justification.
- Expected output: connected spoken turns and sustained pair/group interaction.
- Interaction: pair interview, follow-up questioning, information exchange, small-group discussion.
- Scaffolding: moderate at the beginning, reduced substantially during freer production.
- Autonomy: high during final production.
- Accuracy: functional control of have/has + past participle and appropriate use of Past Simple for finished past time.
- Fluency: sustained communication with manageable pauses; priority is maintaining meaning and interaction rather than perfect form.
- Register/pragmatics: neutral conversational English; learners respond naturally to follow-up questions.
- Assessment criteria: task achievement, appropriate target-language use, coherence, fluency, interaction, and functional accuracy.

## Lesson Result

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to discuss personal experiences using the Present Perfect, ask follow-up questions, and add relevant details using the Past Simple when they move from general experience to a specific finished event.

## Evidence of Learning

Students complete a semi-structured experience interview and a follow-up discussion in which they must:

1. Ask at least four experience questions.
2. Respond with more than Yes/No answers.
3. Ask at least two follow-up questions.
4. Give specific past details when appropriate.
5. Maintain a short conversation without reading a complete script.

## Target Language

Present Perfect:
- I have visited Peru.
- She has never tried sushi.
- Have you ever worked abroad?
- I haven't been to Canada.

Follow-up with Past Simple:
- When did you go?
- Who did you go with?
- What did you do there?
- I went in 2023 and stayed for two weeks.

Useful interaction:
- Really? What was it like?
- How did you feel about it?
- Would you do it again? Why?
- I've never done that. I'd like to try it.
- I've had a similar experience, but...

## 90-Minute Procedure

### 1. Opening and Personal Activation - 8 minutes

Teacher displays five experience prompts: travel, food, work/study, entertainment, challenge.

Students silently choose two experiences they have had and one they have not had. They do not yet explain them.

Pairs compare choices using simple prompts:
- Have you ever...?
- Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.

Purpose: activate meaning before formal explanation.

### 2. Context and Noticing - 10 minutes

Students read Conversation 1 and identify:
- experiences mentioned
- questions about experience
- specific past-time details

They underline Present Perfect examples and circle Past Simple examples.

### Conversation 1 - Accessible B1 Model

A: Have you ever worked in another country?
B: Yes, I have. I worked in the United States for a few years.
A: Really? When did you go?
B: I went in 2016. It was a big change for me.
A: What was the experience like?
B: It was challenging at first, but I learned a lot and met people from many different backgrounds.
A: Would you do it again?
B: Definitely. In fact, I'd like to work abroad again someday.

Purpose: show the difference between general experience and a specific finished event.

### 3. Language Focus - 12 minutes

#### Meaning and Use

Use the Present Perfect when the past experience is connected to the person's experience up to now and the exact finished time is not the focus.

Use the Past Simple when talking about a finished event at a specific past time.

Compare:
- I have visited Mexico. -> experience in my life
- I visited Mexico in 2022. -> specific finished time

#### Form

Affirmative:
- I/You/We/They have + past participle.
- He/She/It has + past participle.

Negative:
- I/You/We/They haven't + past participle.
- He/She/It hasn't + past participle.

Questions:
- Have you ever + past participle...?
- Has she ever + past participle...?

Short answers:
- Yes, I have.
- No, I haven't.
- Yes, she has.
- No, she hasn't.

#### B1 Functional Expansion

Students notice that Present Perfect is not only a question-and-answer formula. It opens a conversation about experience. Follow-up questions then develop the topic.

Example:
- Have you ever visited another country?
- Yes, I have.
- Where did you go?
- I went to Mexico in 2024.
- What did you like about it?
- I loved the food, especially the local dishes.

### 4. Controlled Practice - 10 minutes

Complete the sentences with the correct form of the verb.

1. I ______ never ______ (try) scuba diving.
2. Maria ______ ______ (visit) three countries.
3. ______ you ever ______ (meet) a famous person?
4. We ______ not ______ (see) that movie yet.
5. He ______ ______ (work) abroad before.
6. I ______ (go) to Spain in 2022.
7. They ______ never ______ (eat) Korean food.
8. ______ she ever ______ (drive) a motorcycle?

Then students explain why each example uses Present Perfect or Past Simple.

### 5. Guided Communicative Practice - 15 minutes

#### Experience Interview

Each student receives the same six topic categories but chooses the actual questions.

Categories:
- travel
- food
- work/study
- sports/adventure
- entertainment
- learning something new

Rule: after each Yes answer, the interviewer must ask one follow-up question.

Suggested follow-ups:
- When did you do it?
- Where did it happen?
- Who were you with?
- What happened?
- What was it like?
- Would you do it again? Why?

Students change partners once.

Teacher monitors without interrupting fluency unless communication breaks down.

### 6. Conversation 2 - 8 minutes

Students read and then perform the second model, noticing how the speakers compare experiences and respond naturally.

A: Have you ever learned something that was really difficult?
B: Yes, I have. I've learned to play the guitar, but it took me a long time.
A: How long have you played?
B: I've played for about three years, although I stopped for a few months.
A: What made you start?
B: I heard a friend playing and decided I wanted to learn. Have you ever learned an instrument?
A: Yes. I learned the piano when I was a child, but I haven't played it for years.
B: Would you like to start again?
A: Maybe. I think I'd enjoy it now more than I did then.

B1 feature: the conversation includes follow-up questions, connected responses, comparison, and spontaneous reaction rather than a fixed interview script.

### 7. Freer Production - 17 minutes

#### Three Experiences, One Story

Groups of three discuss experiences from three categories selected by the teacher.

Each student must choose one experience they have had and tell the group:

1. the general experience using Present Perfect;
2. one specific event using Past Simple;
3. one opinion or reflection about the experience.

Example:

"I've traveled alone. I went to Ecuador in 2023 and stayed there for ten days. At first I was nervous, but afterwards I felt much more independent."

Group members must ask follow-up questions and identify one experience they share.

No complete script is provided.

### 8. Feedback and Consolidation - 5 minutes

Teacher gives delayed feedback using anonymized examples from the class.

Students correct selected examples in pairs.

Feedback priorities:
- have/has selection
- past participle
- Present Perfect vs Past Simple meaning
- question formation
- interaction problems that affected communication

Fluency is not interrupted unnecessarily during the communicative stages.

### 9. Exit Evidence - 5 minutes

Each student speaks to a partner for approximately one minute:

"Tell your partner about an experience you have had that changed you, surprised you, or taught you something."

Partner must ask one follow-up question.

Teacher samples performance using the criteria below.

## Assessment Criteria

| Criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Task achievement | Communicates a relevant experience and develops it with details |
| Target language | Uses Present Perfect appropriately and contrasts with Past Simple when relevant |
| Coherence | Connects ideas clearly enough to follow the story |
| Fluency | Maintains communication with manageable hesitation |
| Interaction | Responds to and asks follow-up questions |
| Accuracy | Shows functional control of target forms |

The assessment does not require perfect grammar. The central question is whether the learner can successfully communicate the intended meaning while demonstrating the target language at an appropriate B1 level.

## B1 Quality-Control Audit

### G1 - Level
PASS. The lesson requires sustained communication, explanation, follow-up questions, comparison, reflection, and reduced scripting. B1 is not represented merely through harder vocabulary.

### G2 - Alignment
PASS. The learning result, practice sequence, communicative production, and exit evidence all target experience-sharing and interaction.

### G3 - Language
PASS. Present Perfect and Past Simple functions are accurately distinguished. Examples are appropriate for B1.

### G4 - Communication
PASS. Pair and group tasks require information exchange, follow-up questions, personal meaning, and responses to partners. Students cannot complete the main task by simply reciting a fixed script.

### G5 - Feasibility
PASS. The lesson totals 90 minutes and includes realistic transitions and repeated speaking opportunities. Materials are fully specified.

## Runtime Test Verdict

**PASS - B1 Modo ESL activation produced a genuinely B1-oriented lesson.**

The strongest evidence is the shift from isolated Present Perfect question/answer practice toward sustained interaction: learners narrate experiences, move between Present Perfect and Past Simple for meaning, ask follow-up questions, compare experiences, and express reflection. This satisfies the B1 Level Control requirements for sustained communication, explanation/narration, supporting opinions, interaction management, fluency, and connected discourse.

## Limitations

This is a controlled runtime test of the integrated design rules as applied in conversation. It is not an automated software execution test. Classroom observation with real learners remains necessary for empirical validation of timing, difficulty, and performance.
