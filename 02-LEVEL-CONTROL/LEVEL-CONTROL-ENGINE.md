# LEVEL CONTROL ENGINE

## Version
1.0

## Purpose

The Level Control Engine determines how the same pedagogical topic, language function, grammar point, skill, or communicative task must be adapted for learners at A0, A1, A2, B1, and B2.

The engine does not treat level as a vocabulary label. A level decision must affect the learning objective, linguistic complexity, cognitive demand, interaction, scaffolding, learner autonomy, accuracy expectations, fluency expectations, register, task type, and assessment criteria.

## Core Principle

The same topic does not produce the same lesson at every level.

A topic may remain constant while the pedagogical demand changes substantially.

Example:

Topic: personal experiences

- A0: identify and produce very short supported statements about familiar personal information.
- A1: exchange simple information about routines and past events using highly controlled language.
- A2: describe experiences and events with basic connected language.
- B1: narrate and compare experiences, giving reasons and relevant details.
- B2: discuss experiences critically, explain implications, justify opinions, and manage interaction with greater independence.

## Level Bands

### A0 - Initial / Pre-beginner

Learners may have little or no usable English.

Primary priorities:
- survival communication
- comprehension of very simple classroom language
- recognition and production of essential chunks
- high-frequency vocabulary directly needed for the task
- strong visual, oral, gestural, and contextual support
- repetition with meaningful variation
- confidence and willingness to communicate

Language:
- very short phrases and sentences
- highly controlled structures
- limited grammatical load
- frequent modeling

Interaction:
- teacher modeling
- choral and individual repetition
- pair practice with strong scaffolding
- information exchange using visible language support

Assessment:
- successful communication of the target meaning
- intelligibility
- emerging control of the target language
- participation and ability to complete the communicative task

A0 materials must not assume prior English knowledge that has not been explicitly taught.

### A1 - Beginner

Learners can communicate basic personal and everyday meanings using familiar language.

Primary priorities:
- basic functional communication
- high-frequency structures
- short exchanges
- predictable everyday situations
- gradual reduction of scaffolding

Language:
- simple sentences
- basic questions and answers
- common time, place, people, and activity language
- limited but growing grammatical control

Interaction:
- guided pair work
- short dialogues
- simple information gaps
- controlled-to-guided speaking

Assessment:
- ability to communicate basic intended meanings
- comprehensibility
- appropriate use of target forms in familiar contexts

### A2 - Elementary

Learners can handle routine communication and produce simple connected language.

Primary priorities:
- expanding functional range
- connecting ideas
- describing events, people, places, and experiences
- increasing independence

Language:
- short connected sentences
- wider range of common structures
- basic discourse markers
- increasing grammatical accuracy without sacrificing communication

Interaction:
- information gaps
- role plays
- short discussions
- guided problem-solving
- personal and experience-based tasks

Assessment:
- successful completion of communicative tasks
- basic organization and connection of ideas
- intelligibility and functional grammatical control

### B1 - Intermediate

Learners can communicate independently in familiar and moderately unfamiliar situations.

Primary priorities:
- sustained communication
- explanation and narration
- expressing and supporting opinions
- handling unexpected but manageable interaction
- increasing fluency and flexibility

Language:
- connected discourse
- broader grammatical and lexical range
- appropriate use of common complex structures
- functional variation in register

Interaction:
- discussions
- role plays with less scripting
- problem-solving
- presentations
- collaborative tasks

Assessment:
- task achievement
- coherence
- fluency
- range and control
- ability to maintain interaction

### B2 - Upper-intermediate

Learners can communicate effectively and with increasing precision across a broad range of contexts.

Primary priorities:
- nuanced communication
- argumentation
- critical thinking
- flexibility of expression
- register awareness
- spontaneous interaction

Language:
- complex and varied structures
- precise vocabulary and collocation
- cohesive discourse
- appropriate formal, neutral, and informal choices
- meaningful attention to subtle differences in form and meaning

Interaction:
- extended discussions
- debates
- problem-solving with competing priorities
- presentations followed by questions
- analysis and synthesis tasks

Assessment:
- effectiveness and precision
- coherence and cohesion
- fluency and interaction management
- grammatical and lexical range/control
- register and pragmatic appropriacy

## Adaptation Matrix

For every lesson or activity, the Director Pedagógico must check at least these dimensions:

| Dimension | A0 | A1 | A2 | B1 | B2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Objective | Immediate survival/recognition | Basic functional use | Routine independent use | Independent communication | Precise, flexible communication |
| Language load | Minimal | Low | Moderate | Moderate-high | High |
| Cognitive demand | Recognition/reproduction | Selection/application | Combination | Analysis/application | Analysis/evaluation/synthesis |
| Scaffolding | Very high | High | Moderate | Selective | Minimal/strategic |
| Interaction | Highly controlled | Guided | Semi-guided | Increasingly free | Mostly spontaneous |
| Output | Words/chunks/simple sentences | Simple sentences/short exchanges | Connected short discourse | Sustained discourse | Extended, nuanced discourse |
| Accuracy expectation | Emerging | Basic control | Functional control | Generally controlled | High precision when required |
| Fluency expectation | Emerging | Short supported turns | Connected simple speech | Sustained communication | Flexible, spontaneous communication |
| Register | Basic neutral language | Basic everyday register | Everyday + simple situational variation | Register awareness begins to matter | Register is an explicit performance dimension |
| Autonomy | Very low | Low | Moderate | High | High |

## Level Adaptation Rules

1. Never adapt a lesson by changing vocabulary alone.
2. Preserve the communicative purpose while adjusting the demand.
3. Increase cognitive demand as proficiency increases.
4. Reduce scaffolding progressively as learner autonomy increases.
5. Increase output length only when the learner's control and task purpose justify it.
6. Do not confuse complexity with quality. A higher-level lesson must be more demanding, not merely longer.
7. Do not penalize lower-level learners for not demonstrating higher-level features that were not taught or required.
8. At B2, precision, nuance, register, cohesion, and pragmatic appropriacy may become explicit objectives.
9. At A0, communicative success must not depend on knowledge that has not been taught.
10. The same grammar item may have different objectives at different levels. The engine must define the function and cognitive demand, not simply the form.

## Controlled-to-Free Progression

The default progression is:

1. Input and meaning
2. Model and noticing
3. Controlled practice
4. Guided practice
5. Communicative practice
6. Freer production
7. Feedback and reflection

The proportion changes by level.

A0 and A1 require more modeling and controlled/guided practice. A2 requires a stronger transition toward independent communication. B1 and B2 should prioritize meaningful production and interaction, using controlled work strategically rather than as the dominant lesson mode.

## Level Validation Gate

Before a lesson is approved, the Quality Control Engine must ask:

- Is the stated level appropriate?
- Does the objective match the level?
- Is the language load appropriate?
- Is the cognitive demand appropriate?
- Is the amount of scaffolding appropriate?
- Is the expected learner output realistic?
- Is the interaction type appropriate?
- Are assessment criteria aligned with the level?
- Has the lesson accidentally introduced higher-level language demands?
- Has the lesson been made artificially simplistic by lowering only vocabulary?

If any answer is negative, the product must be revised before classroom delivery.

## Level Control Output

When the user requests a lesson, sequence, activity, worksheet, assessment, or project and specifies a level, the system should internally establish:

- target level
- learner profile
- communicative objective
- language objective
- cognitive demand
- expected output
- interaction type
- scaffolding level
- autonomy level
- accuracy expectation
- fluency expectation
- register/pragmatics where relevant
- assessment criteria

These decisions must influence the final classroom-ready product.

## Priority Rule

If a user request conflicts with the level requirements, the Director Pedagógico should preserve the pedagogical intent while adapting the task to make it genuinely appropriate for the requested level.

The engine exists to prevent "same lesson, easier vocabulary" design.
